# Avery Quote Tool — independent audit, October 12, 2026

The latest version is a substantial, working internal comparison tool. Its pricing and document safeguards are useful. However, several paths still produce wrong quantities or allow unresolved source/specification problems into a confirmed customer summary. Fix the P1 findings before treating confirmation as a dependable gate for routine customer use.

Audited baseline: `0156d77629f6c596333ea2f6b4207237ab8ea917`, app v0.9.0, committed October 7. The checkout was clean at audit start. Read-only `git ls-remote` confirmed that GitHub HEAD and main point to this same commit. The handoff and early improvement-plan statements that this work is uncommitted and exists only on this laptop are stale. Hosted deployment state was not verified.

## Findings to fix

P1 means a correction is needed before relying on the affected workflow for customer comparisons. P2 means an important usability or operational defect. All line references below refer to the audited baseline.

### F1 — P1: total footage is insufficient to calculate continuous cuts

**Evidence:** [requirements.js:62](/Users/inspgadget/Documents/Hitlights/avery-quote-tool/lib/requirements.js:62) computes `ceil(totalFt / rollFt)` with a tolerance. Three source pieces of **10 ft**, against **16.4 ft** rolls, produce **two rolls**. Two rolls cannot supply three continuous 10 ft cuts. One source piece longer than a roll is also accepted without a splice/design decision.

**Impact:** purchase quantity can be insufficient even though total footage appears correct. This matters whenever source lengths represent separate continuous runs; it is not an issue for a pure bulk-footage requirement that explicitly permits joining material.

**Correction:** distinguish bulk length from number of continuous cuts. Calculate feasible cuts per roll, block/flag over-length runs, and require an explicit reviewed splice/design assumption where applicable. Keep rounding cases after calculating required rolls. Do not infer that all offcuts are interchangeable.

### F2 — P1: quantity edits reuse a rounded multiplier instead of recalculating

**Evidence:** [review.js:32](/Users/inspgadget/Documents/Hitlights/avery-quote-tool/lib/review.js:32). Three 7 ft pieces initially produce two rolls. Changing source quantity to six while leaving the displayed Avery count alone saves **four rolls** through `6 × (2/3)`. The current conversion function gives **three rolls**, and three 16.4 ft rolls can supply six 7 ft cuts. Changing source quantity from three to four instead fails save with a fractional Avery quantity.

**Impact:** normal review edits can inflate cost, produce misleading savings, or reject an otherwise valid edit. Selecting another tape SKU also retains the old roll count instead of deriving it from the replacement roll length.

**Correction:** use the same requirement conversion in matching and review after source-quantity or replacement changes. Represent a manual override explicitly, with its explanation; do not reuse a ratio derived from an already-rounded quantity. Regenerate the visible working.

### F3 — P1: unresolved tape quantities can be confirmed unchanged

**Evidence:** [quotes.js:64](/Users/inspgadget/Documents/Hitlights/avery-quote-tool/lib/quotes.js:64) leaves `qty × multiplier` as the replacement quantity when tape length conversion fails and merely sets low confidence. [review.js:36](/Users/inspgadget/Documents/Hitlights/avery-quote-tool/lib/review.js:36) gates confirmation on `assessMatch().gaps`, which does not include failed length conversion. A line with known 24V/CCT/brightness but **no source length** was confirmed with its fallback count and **blank customer note**, after checking the two general review attestations.

**Impact:** a warning to enter roll count manually does not actually require a reviewed manual quantity. Unknown brightness has a similar gap: a low-confidence matcher reason alone does not require a specification note.

**Correction:** persist structured unresolved requirements and enforce their resolution at confirmation. Require a documented manual quantity decision when conversion is impossible; keep low confidence and material unknowns visible in the customer document until resolved.

### F4 — P1: successful AI extraction discards deterministic price conflicts

**Evidence:** [extract.js:177](/Users/inspgadget/Documents/Hitlights/avery-quote-tool/lib/extract.js:177). A source row printed as `$10 × 5 = $60` correctly becomes unpriced with a warning through the rules reader. A mocked AI returning the same row count and a $10 unit price wins extraction; the rules warning is dropped and the item becomes priced. There is no field-level reconciliation with the rules result.

**Impact:** enabling AI weakens a safeguard that works with AI off. An overall-total warning may catch some cases but does not resolve which line/price is wrong, and documents without a recognized total lose even that signal.

**Correction:** preserve deterministic warnings on either extraction path and reconcile source row identity, quantities and unit/extended prices. A successful AI reply must not silently clear an arithmetic contradiction.

### F5 — P1: source-total mismatches do not require resolution

**Evidence:** [review.js:55](/Users/inspgadget/Documents/Hitlights/avery-quote-tool/lib/review.js:55). An extracted **$100** driver line with a printed **$200** quote total produces a mismatch banner. The quote still reaches `done` after ordinary review checks, without correcting the discrepancy or recording why it exists. The confirmed summary contains only the extracted scope and cannot list a line that extraction missed. The banner also is not recomputed when source prices/quantities are edited.

**Impact:** a missing product can disappear from the comparison while confirmation implies that the source was reviewed. Legitimate tax/freight/discount differences also remain indistinguishable from missing merchandise.

**Correction:** reconcile product subtotal and separately model source charges/discounts. Require correction or an explicit reviewed explanation for unresolved differences. Recompute after edits. Add manual source-line insertion so a missing line can be fixed without re-reading everything.

### F6 — P1: the wet-location check treats distinct protection ratings as equivalent

**Evidence:** [match-safety.js:15](/Users/inspgadget/Documents/Hitlights/avery-quote-tool/lib/match-safety.js:15). A source explicitly specifying **IP68** matched to an **IP65** catalog item produces **no blocker and no gap**. The check accepts any target matching its broad wet-rating regex. Current IP20 tape would be rejected, so this defect becomes relevant when a wet-rated replacement is present in the catalog.

**Impact:** an explicit environmental specification difference can be omitted entirely. The current checker does not establish that a particular replacement meets the specified exposure.

**Correction:** preserve the actual environmental requirement and flag differences pending verified manufacturer/application evidence. Do not treat every recognized wet rating as interchangeable or infer suitability from a simple numeric ordering.

### F7 — P2: advertised upload capacity exceeds the request limit

**Evidence:** [app/page.js:17](/Users/inspgadget/Documents/Hitlights/avery-quote-tool/app/page.js:17) accepts five PDFs of up to 10 MB each, while [next.config.mjs:4](/Users/inspgadget/Documents/Hitlights/avery-quote-tool/next.config.mjs:4) limits the entire server-action request to **20 MB**. A valid five-file batch can exceed the limit before the upload function runs. This is a code/configuration finding; oversized upload was not exercised in the browser.

**Correction:** make the aggregate limit explicit and enforce it in the upload UI, or use an upload path consistent with the intended capacity. Framework limits apply to the whole multipart request, including overhead. [Next.js serverActions documentation](https://nextjs.org/docs/app/api-reference/config/next-config-js/serverActions).

### F8 — P2: per-metre calculations print a feet label

**Evidence:** [requirements.js:30](/Users/inspgadget/Documents/Hitlights/avery-quote-tool/lib/requirements.js:30) uses `perFoot: true` for metre-priced source lines. [requirements.js:67](/Users/inspgadget/Documents/Hitlights/avery-quote-tool/lib/requirements.js:67) consequently prints **“10 ft = 32.81 ft”** for ten source metres. The roll arithmetic uses metres correctly; the explanation saved to the customer summary is wrong.

**Correction:** carry the actual source unit and display “10 m = 32.81 ft.” Add a customer-text assertion for metre-priced input.

### F9 — P2: mobile purchase quantities are difficult to read

**Evidence:** the confirmed demo summary at **390 px** viewport width compresses the purchase table's SKU/product column to roughly one character per line. The two-product demo becomes a several-screen vertical table. The comparison table also squeezes descriptions and its last column. Desktop rendering is readable.

**Correction:** use a horizontally scrollable table with a sensible minimum width, or a compact mobile card layout. Validate actual reading of SKU, required rolls, pack count and cost on a phone, not merely the absence of a viewport overflow error.

## Recommended next work

1. **Correct quantities and enforce review:** F1–F3 together. Establish continuous-cut versus bulk-length semantics, use one conversion path for edits/matches, and require explicit unresolved/manual decisions.
2. **Keep source evidence authoritative:** F4–F6. Preserve conflict warnings across AI extraction, resolve totals with source-line correction, and retain actual environmental requirements.
3. **Verify with real inputs:** representative competitor PDFs plus the real catalog and human expected answers. Include missing lines, per-metre pricing, continuous cuts and mismatched totals. Run in an isolated staging database.
4. **Finish the smaller usability fixes:** F7–F9. Then decide on background jobs, server-generated PDFs, named reviewers and backup/restore work according to expected usage.

Keep the current whole-pack default while these corrections are made. Loose-piece availability and commercial terms still require business evidence; the audit does not change those assumptions.
